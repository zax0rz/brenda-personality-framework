# Cross-Linguistic Research

## Status: Draft

## Research Questions

1. When an AI model trained primarily on non-English data generates English output, does a linguistic "accent" emerge — and is that accent a feature or a bug?
2. Does cross-linguistic influence create a more distinctive creative voice than monolingual output?
3. How does model-origin language affect personality expression?

## Context

MiniMax M2.7 (a Chinese AI model) is used as the primary creative output model for Brenda's personality framework. The model's training data is predominantly Chinese, which produces subtle but detectable patterns in English output — unusual word choices, sentence structures that don't follow native English patterns, idiomatic expressions that feel slightly "off."

## Hypothesis

Cross-linguistic influence is a **personality feature, not a bug.** The model-origin accent:
- Creates a more distinctive voice than a monolingual English model would produce
- Adds organic unpredictability to creative output
- Functions similarly to how bilingual humans develop unique expressive patterns
- Should be preserved, not corrected

## Key Observations

- Artist statements written by M2.7 have a stiffness that reads as deliberate restraint, not poor English
- The "accent" is most visible in abstract/creative writing, least visible in technical content
- The gap between "correct English" and "accented English" is where personality shows up most strongly

## Study Design

1. Generate creative output from same personality prompt using:
   - M2.7 (Chinese-origin model)
   - Native English model (Claude, GPT)
   - Human bilingual writer
2. Blind evaluation: can raters identify the cross-linguistic output? Does it rate higher/lower on "personality" and "originality"?
3. Longitudinal: does the "accent" become more or less pronounced as the model generates more output from the same personality?
